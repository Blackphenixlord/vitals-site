NASA HUNCH 2026–27 · SOFTWARE & TECHNOLOGY

When the doctor is twenty minutes away.

VITALS is a medical decision-support system for long-duration crews. A crewmember describes how they feel in their own words. VITALS says what it could be, how serious it is, what to do now, and which NASA source that comes from — with no internet connection.

How it works See the screens

Decision support, not a diagnosis. A student project, not a medical device.

Far from Earth, asking for help takes time a crew may not have.

3–22 min One-way signal delay between Earth and Mars. A question and its answer can take most of an hour.
Offline No search engine, no cloud AI. Everything VITALS needs runs on one small computer on board.
Plain words Crew describe symptoms the way people really talk — not in clinical terms — and VITALS asks one follow-up at a time.

HOW IT WORKS

A language model that has to show its sources.

01 Listen The complaint is read for findings — pain, where, how bad, flight day — and shown back so the crew can fix anything misread.
02 Narrow A knowledge base of 14 spaceflight conditions and 72 findings, each tied to NASA and peer-reviewed sources, picks what could fit.
03 Reason A local model (Llama 3.2 on Ollama) ranks only those conditions. Anything it invents is dropped and reported.
04 Double-check A separate rule engine always votes on how serious it is. If it raises the alarm, the answer raises the alarm.
05 Answer Emergency, urgent, monitor or routine — as a word and a color — then what to do, and the source link for every condition.
Citations are never written by the model. It returns an id; the link comes from the knowledge base. No percentages on screen. Confidence is shown in words — the old numbers weren't defensible. Same question, same answer. The model runs at temperature 0, so every behavior can be tested.

THE SCREENS

Built for gloves, small panels and bad light.

Every tap target is at least 44 px. Text passes 4.5:1 contrast. The page is one file that loads with the network cable pulled.
